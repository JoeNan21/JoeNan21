# REAL-CASE-002 — evidence interview

Reconstruct only what was known **on or before** the decision date. Do not
include anything learned afterwards, however minor.

## How to label every item

| Label | Means | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **FACT** | Documented or directly observed | Name the source (invoice, contract, email, photo, roster, your own observation on a date) |
| **INFERENCE** | You worked it out from other things you knew | Say which facts it came from |
| **ASSUMPTION** | You believed it without evidence | Say so plainly; assumptions are useful and are not penalised for being assumptions |
| **UNKNOWN** | You genuinely did not know | Give criticality: low / medium / high |

If you are unsure whether something is a FACT or an INFERENCE, call it an
INFERENCE. Over-claiming is the error that damages the case; under-claiming is not.

Short answers are fine. "Not known" and "no record" are valid and useful answers
— they become UNKNOWNs, which the engine treats as first-class.

## 0. Establishing `as_of` — required before anything else

The whole case is checked against this date. A claim dated after it is rejected
as hindsight.

- **0.1** What event marks the decision point — the moment you had the
  information and the decision was live, but before you had settled it?
- **0.2** What is the date of that event (YYYY-MM-DD)? Approximate is
  acceptable if you say so; err **earlier** rather than later, since an
  `as_of` set too late admits hindsight.
- **0.3** What is the source that fixes that date (an invoice, an email, a
  calendar entry, a meeting)?
- **0.4** How material was this decision at the time: low / medium / high?
- **0.5** Was there a deadline or forcing event, or was the timing open?

## 1. Scope and contract

- **1.1** What form did the arrangement take — written contract, verbal
  understanding, inherited practice, something else? What documents existed?
- **1.2** What was the stated or understood scope of work?
- **1.3** What frequency or schedule was expected?
- **1.4** Were there defined deliverables, standards or acceptance criteria?
- **1.5** What were the commercial terms — rate basis, notice period, minimum
  term, any exit provisions?
- **1.6** Which of the above were documented, and which were understood without
  documentation?

## 2. Invoices and cost

- **2.1** What was actually being invoiced, and on what basis and frequency?
- **2.2** Over what period, and what was the total across it?
- **2.3** Did invoiced amounts vary? If so, was the variation explained?
- **2.4** Did invoices itemise work performed, or were they summary?
- **2.5** How did the cost sit against the wider grounds/maintenance budget?
- **2.6** Was any comparison figure available at the time — a benchmark,
  historical rate, or budget line?

## 3. Work actually observed

Neutral by design. Report what was seen, including work that met expectations.

- **3.1** On what occasions did you or anyone at Sorrento directly observe work
  being performed? Dates, if known.
- **3.2** What was observed on those occasions?
- **3.3** Were there periods where work was expected and attendance was not
  observed? Were there periods where it was observed as expected?
- **3.4** Was there any record — photos, logs, sign-in, staff reports?
- **3.5** What was the observable condition of the grounds, and how did it
  change over the period?
- **3.6** Was the condition attributable to this arrangement, or were other
  factors involved (weather, season, other contractors, in-house work)?

## 4. Communications with the supplier

- **4.1** What contact had there been, in what form, and how often?
- **4.2** Had scope, cost or performance been raised? What was said, and when?
- **4.3** How were queries responded to — content and responsiveness?
- **4.4** Had any change to the arrangement been proposed by either side?
- **4.5** Was there anything on record (email, message, note), or was it verbal?

## 5. Service expectations

- **5.1** What did Sorrento actually need from grounds maintenance?
- **5.2** Where had those expectations come from — a predecessor, a contract,
  your own judgement, guest or client feedback?
- **5.3** Were expectations ever stated to the supplier, or assumed?
- **5.4** What would "performing well" have looked like, concretely?

## 6. Event requirements

- **6.1** What did the events calendar require of the grounds in the relevant
  period — volume, type, and any dates with heightened requirements?
- **6.2** Had grounds condition affected any event, enquiry, site visit or piece
  of client feedback? In either direction?
- **6.3** Were there upcoming commitments that constrained the timing of any
  change?
- **6.4** What would a lapse in grounds maintenance have cost commercially, if
  anything, and is that documented or estimated?

## 7. Alternative suppliers and quotes

Only what was known **at the time**.

- **7.1** Were any alternative suppliers known, identified or approached before
  the decision point?
- **7.2** Had any quote, indicative price or scope proposal been received? What
  did it say, and on what date?
- **7.3** Was the local market for this service known — availability, typical
  rates, lead times?
- **7.4** If no alternatives were explored, was that a decision or simply where
  things stood?

## 8. Relationship considerations

- **8.1** How did the arrangement come to Sorrento — inherited from whom, and
  on what basis?
- **8.2** Was there any relationship between the supplier and ownership,
  management, neighbours or the wider community?
- **8.3** Was there any history, obligation or expectation attached to it?
- **8.4** Would changing the arrangement have had consequences beyond the
  commercial ones?
- **8.5** How long had the arrangement been running, as far as was known?

## 9. Switching risks

- **9.1** What would replacing the supplier have involved practically —
  notice, handover, equipment, site knowledge, access?
- **9.2** How quickly could a replacement plausibly have started?
- **9.3** What would have happened to the grounds in any gap?
- **9.4** Were there contractual or financial consequences to ending it early?
- **9.5** Were there risks in *continuing* unchanged? State them as evidence,
  not as conclusions.

## 10. Genuine unknowns

For each: the question, its criticality (low / medium / high), and which
options it blocked.

- **10.1** What did you not know about the work actually performed?
- **10.2** What did you not know about the cost's reasonableness?
- **10.3** What did you not know about the contractual position?
- **10.4** What did you not know about the alternatives?
- **10.5** What did you not know about consequences of changing it?
- **10.6** Anything else genuinely open at the decision point?

## 11. Commercial constraints

- **11.1** Was there a budget constraint, and was a figure attached?
- **11.2** Who had authority to change or end the arrangement? Was that clear
  at the time?
- **11.3** Did anyone else need to approve or be consulted?
- **11.4** Was there a time constraint on deciding?
- **11.5** What else was competing for attention and spend at that point?

## Before you send

The context you provide must be one from which **a reader could not tell which
option was taken**. If it reads as a case for a particular course of action, it
is contaminated and the score will be worthless.

Two specific checks:

- Include the evidence that pointed the other way. Whatever the decision was,
  something argued against it, and that evidence is what makes the case worth
  scoring.
- Sections 3, 4 and 5 are where hindsight most easily enters. Report what was
  observed and said, not what it later came to mean.

**Do not include the decision or the outcome.** Those come after Arm A has run
and its recommendation is committed.
